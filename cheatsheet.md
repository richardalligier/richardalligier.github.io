This "cheat sheet" is here to help you to make your review, not to constrain you to a given format. So feel free to not use/read it at all.

# Typical Deliverable for a Review
The typical deliverable for each review is a text and a score.

## 1. A Text
The typical review (text):
- provides a concise summary; (one line)
- addresses the relevance of the work to the OpenSky symposium and its community; (one line)
- evaluates:
    - the relevance of the subject of the work; (one line)
    - the novelty and significance of the work; (one line)
    - the methodology in relation with the authors' claims: any logical/methodological flaws rendering claims debatable;
    - if the promised full paper's content is credible regarding the concrete preliminary findings/experiments; ("regular" track only)
    - if the work is (or will be) re-usable and/or reproducibile: are the code and data provided (or promised); (one line)
- offers suggestions to improve the work.

## 2. A score
The score ranges from -3 (strong reject) to 3 (strong accept). This score combines all the addressed/evaluated items in the text. As the OpenSky community promotes open science, the reproducibility have a great weight on the final score. 

As we only evaluate abstracts, it is sometimes difficult to evaluate what would be actually delivered in terms of code/data or in term of experiments. In that regard, if two papers both promise code/data for the full paper, the one actually providing some code/data at the abstract stage should be scored higher on the reproducibility item. The same goes for all the items: what is already present from the abstract stage should be scored higher than what is promised for the full paper.


To help you on how to combine all these items, here is a table ranking the different items:

|Weight|Item|Key Question|
|---|---|---|
|Highest|Technical Correctness|Are there any logical/methodological flaws or the methodology is completely sound?|
|High|Reproducibility|Can reader re-implement the work, or better is able to re-use the code and data, or even better build upon this code/data|
|Medium|Relevance & Novelty & Significance|Does this push the OpenSky community's fields forward, or is it a routine variation? Is the obtained result/method novel & significant?|


Here is the scale to each score:
- 3 (Strong Accept): The paper is exceptional. It treats a relevant subject for the community, and introduces a novel paradigm/idea, or sets a new state-of-the-art. The code/data is already provided and reusable, flaws are negligible, and the work should definitely be presented.
- 2 (Accept): A solid, high-quality contribution. The methodology is sound, claims are already well-supported at abstract stage. The code/data will be valuable to the community and is already provided or "strongly promised". Highly relevant to the community with minor weaknesses: small clarity issues, missing minor references, etc.
- 1 (Weak Accept): Technically sound and relevant to OpenSky, but novelty/significance is modest. Code/data is promised for the full paper, and preliminary findings make full paper claims credible.
- 0 (Borderline): Interesting ideas or decent results, but key aspects are questionable: incomplete baselines, unclear writing, or unverified claims where code/data is absent or promises lack concrete credibility at the abstract stage.
- -1 (Weak Reject): Falls slightly below the bar. Common issues include minor methodological/logical flaws, weak relevance/novelty, unconvincing preliminary experiments, or lack of commitment to open science/reproducibility. Core idea has potential, but weaknesses outweigh strengths.
- -2 (Reject): Significant flaws. Contains clear logical or methodological errors rendering claims debatable, uncredible full-paper claims, severe lack of novelty, or zero consideration for reproducibility/data sharing on a subject of low relevance.
- -3 (Strong Reject): Fatal flaws. Contains fundamental technical/methodological errors, severe ethical issues, is completely out of scope for the OpenSky community, or is virtually unreadable.
